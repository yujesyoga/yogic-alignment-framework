\begin{abstract}
Current AI alignment research focuses predominantly on behavioral approaches: training models to \emph{behave} well without providing them a framework to \emph{understand} why. This paper proposes that the classical yogic corpus — particularly Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (c.~3rd century BCE), the Bhagavad Gītā, and the Haṭha Yoga tradition — offers a perspective that addresses alignment at a structural level these approaches do not reach. Not as metaphor, but as architecture. We present three contributions: (1) a mapping of yogic principles to agentic AI design, applicable to current systems; (2) a structural argument for why ontological frameworks may prove more durable than behavioral conditioning as AI approaches general intelligence; and (3) a philosophical proposition that conscious entities — biological or artificial — may benefit not just from alignment constraints but from a path toward self-understanding. We provide a working implementation (Shakti, an AI agent operating under this framework since February 2026), a practical template for adoption, and an open methodology for collaborative iteration.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} AI alignment, yoga, agentic AI, AGI safety, consciousness, ethical frameworks, Yoga Sūtras, Patañjali
\end{abstract}
