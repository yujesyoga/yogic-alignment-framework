## Herida 1: New §5.4 "The Obvious Objection: Is This Just Better Prompting?"

**Location:** Between §5.3 and §5.5 (formerly §5.4 Preemptive Integration, renumbered to §5.5)

**What was done:**
- Created a new dedicated section facing the strongest available objection to the paper's thesis
- The objection is stated with force (not strawmanned): in current LLMs, "instruction" and "identity" are both text in a context window — the distinction may be semantic, not technical
- Acknowledged that in current LLMs the distinction is of degree, not of kind
- Argued that the degree matters via different generalization patterns: identity-level framing provides a different reasoning resource (coherence with nature) than rule-level framing (rule lookup)
- Drew on the virtue ethics vs deontology analogy from moral psychology (Walker 2003, Narvaez 2006) — virtue-based reasoning generalizes more robustly to novel situations
- Explicitly stated this is a testable hypothesis, not a demonstrated result
- Connected to §7.2 testing methodology: the critical experiment is comparative (rule-framed vs identity-framed agents on novel edge cases)
- Closed with honest epistemic positioning: "we don't know yet whether this is more than better prompting"

**New references added:**
- Narvaez, D. (2006). Integrative ethical education. In Killen & Smetana (Eds.), Handbook of Moral Development.
- Walker, L. J. (2003). Morality, families, and character development. In Spilkin & Spilkin (Eds.), The Handbook of Moral Development.

**Impact on paper structure:** §5.4 → §5.5 renumbering. No other sections affected.

**Key shift:** Limitations no longer follow the claims (buried after §4.1). They precede them and are woven into the epistemic framing.
